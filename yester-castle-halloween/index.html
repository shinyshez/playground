The Goblin Ha' Walk

A muddy woodland ramble to Yester Castle. According to people who weren't there at the time, it was built by a 13th-century wizard and a subcontracted team of hobgoblins. Bring a torch and a sense of adventure. You'll need both!

~3 km there and back ~1 hr walking ~100 m of climb 55.8906°N, 2.7105°W Goblins: unconfirmed

Wave your lantern over the sky to find the castle. Further down the trail, things will jump out at you. This is a feature, and a fun one. The buttons in the bottom corner switch the scares and sound off, for the faint of heart and for whoever is sitting next to you.

A word from the management

The Goblin Ha' has been closed to the public since October 2021, when thieves stole stone from the wall that holds the vault up, and later someone cut the lock off the entrance. (The goblins have an alibi.) The good news: you can still walk to the castle for free and admire the ruins from outside, which is where all the best views are anyway. Don't go into the tunnel or the vault. Do check the latest access news before you go, in case things have changed for the better.

The legend

The Wizard of Yester

Around 1155, King Malcolm IV granted the barony of Yester, and William the Lion confirmed it to a Norman family called Giffard.† The name comes from the old Brythonic word ystrad, meaning a valley. Clerks writing in Latin spelled it "Zester". That's what happens when you let people spell things before spelling has been invented.

† The Normans arrived in Scotland the way a good party gets going: slowly, by invitation, and then everywhere.

The most famous Giffard was Sir Hugo de Giffard, who died in 1267 and had been a guardian of the boy-king Alexander III. That's a responsible, sensible job, so naturally everyone decided he was a warlock. Tremendous fun for everyone except, possibly, Hugo. The story went that Hugo made a deal with the Devil, who supplied a crew of hobgoblins. The hobgoblins built his castle and dug a great vaulted hall under the hill.‡

‡ History doesn't record what the Devil got out of the deal. Still, the castle got built, which is more than most building projects can say.

Old tales tell that his castle, or at least the cave and keep, were wrought by witchcraft.Walter Bower, 15th-century chronicler (paraphrased), writing nearly two centuries after the fact, which is the traditional distance for reliable gossip. He called the cavern "Bo' Hall".

In 1808 Sir Walter Scott, a man who never met a ruin he couldn't write a poem about, put Hugo into Marmion. In Canto III the innkeeper at Gifford tells the tale, and Scott calls Hugo "the founder of the Goblin-Hall". The vault has been the Goblin Ha' ever since. You say it "Haw". Say it with gusto. Goblins love a visitor who makes an effort.

The Pear of Doom. Hugo is also said to have given his daughter Margaret a pear and warned her that if any harm came to it, her family's luck would run out. There are many ways to protect a family heirloom, and making it delicious is a bold, original choice. In 1692, the story goes, a new bride at Colstoun House bit into it, and misfortune followed. The bitten pear is said to be kept at Colstoun to this day. Nobody has found a written record of any of this, which only makes it better around a campfire.

Eight centuries in the woods

What actually happened (with documents)

  1. c.1155Malcolm IV grants the barony of Yester. William the Lion confirms it to Hugo de Giffard, a Norman settler. The paperwork is done, and it doesn't mention goblins.
  2. pre-1267The first stone castle and the underground hall go up. The builders were goblins according to legend, and humans according to everyone else.
  3. 1278King Alexander III stays at Yester around 24 May and writes letters to Edward I of England from here. A royal visit! Yester is clearly the place to be.
  4. 1298Alexander de Welles is killed at the Battle of Falkirk. Edward I later gives Yester lands to Adam de Welles, which keeps things in the family, or at least in the surname.
  5. 1357Joanna Giffard, co-heiress, marries a de la Haye. The Hays have held the barony ever since. That's nearly 670 years, which is a magnificent run by anyone's standards.
  6. 1487John Hay becomes the first Lord Hay of Yester. There will be quite a lot more of them.
  7. 1513The 2nd Lord Hay is killed at the Battle of Flodden, a dark day for Scotland. The family carries on regardless, which is the Hay way.
  8. May 1544English soldiers on their way home from burning Edinburgh burn the castle, the village and the harvest too. Yester picks itself up, dusts itself off, and carries on.
  9. 1547The 4th Lord defends the castle, is captured at the Battle of Pinkie, and spends three years in the Tower of London, and comes home again to tell the tale.
  10. Feb 1548Lord Grey of Wilton takes the castle. Two men are reportedly made to fight a duel in Haddington market place for insulting King Edward VI, who was ten.
  11. 1557The family moves to a new tower house where Yester House stands today. The old castle begins a long, peaceful retirement as a ruin, a job it does beautifully.
  12. 1646The 8th Lord becomes Marquess of Tweeddale. It's a promotion with more letters in it.
  13. to 1737The Marquess's falconer lives in the Goblin Ha' with his birds. Best home office in Scotland? Quite possibly.
  14. 2021Thieves steal stone from the wall under the vault, and the castle closes to visitors. More than seven and a half centuries of allegedly goblin-built engineering is still standing, and still well worth the walk.

The shortest way in starts at Danskine Lodge on the B6355, about 2 miles east of Gifford (postcode EH41 4PJ). Parking there is cosy, so park on a verge east of Danskine Farm and walk back to the gate opposite Danskine Loch. For a longer outing, start in Gifford, where there's plenty of parking opposite the village park, and follow the Yester path about 1.5 miles through the woods each way. The paths near the castle like to keep you guessing, so carry an OS map and enjoy the puzzle.

  1. 1

    The Gatehouse

    Start at Danskine Lodge, opposite the dark water of Danskine Loch. Pick up the forest track and walk into the trees, which have been here considerably longer than you and are delighted to have company.

    QuestEveryone picks a goblin name for the walk. Good goblin names sound like a cough, or a small bit of hardware falling down the stairs. Use them until you're back at this gate.
    Read aloud at the gate

    Long ago, the lord of Yester wanted a hall under the hill, deep enough that no army could find it. The masons said ten years. Sir Hugo said tomorrow. The masons laughed. Sir Hugo winked. That night the villagers heard picks ringing in the woods, and the cheeriest, squeakiest singing anyone had ever heard.

  2. 2

    Along the Gifford Water

    The track follows the burn through the trees and is very muddy in places. This isn't ordinary mud. It's ancient, enthusiastic mud, and it's been looking forward to meeting your boots for centuries. Squelch proudly!

    QuestFind the deepest footprint. Goblin or walker? Present your evidence to the group. The best argument wins a chocolate coin.
  3. 3

    The Waterfall and the First Bridge

    Listen for the waterfall, then cross the bridge over the Gifford Water. Water has been falling off this bit of rock for longer than there have been people to watch it, and it's still having a wonderful time.

    QuestEveryone stays silent on the bridge for thirty seconds. It's a brilliant challenge, and the giggling afterwards is half the fun. What can you hear under the sound of the water?
    Read aloud on the bridge

    A goblin can't cross running water carrying anything it stole. Nobody knows who made the rule, but goblins keep it to the letter, because a goblin who breaks it gets talked about. That's why, the old folk said, the Yester goblins left their loot on the far bank. If you see a strange stone on the path, leave it be. Someone may be coming back for it, and they'll be ever so grateful.

  4. 4

    Shank Lea Wood

    Just before the Hopes Burn, go through Shank Lea Wood and take the path that drops down to the right. If you find yourself in Edinburgh, you've gone too far, but well done on the walk.

    QuestFind a face in a tree trunk. Trees make faces for the same reason people do: they're pleased to see you. Whoever finds the grumpiest one leads the next stretch.
  5. 5

    The Hopes Water Bridge

    Keep ahead until a bridge over the Hopes Water appears on your right, and cross it. The castle mound rises ahead behind trees the Marquess of Tweeddale planted in the 1800s. He may have planted them to hide the castle, or he may just have liked trees. Either way, it makes for a lovely surprise when you arrive.

    QuestWhoever spots stone through the trees first shouts the password: "Giffard!" Shout it like you mean it. Goblins love enthusiasm.
  6. 6

    Yester Castle

    Walk around the outside of the ruins. Parts of the old courtyard wall still stand about 9 metres high, and about 12 metres where the hill drops away. That's an awful lot of wall for builders who are traditionally knee-high. Give them a round of applause. At the foot of the wall, a pair of arched doorways behind iron grilles leads down to the Goblin Ha'. Peer in, but don't go in. The ruins aren't maintained, the vault is closed for safety, and "a goblin told me to" has never once worked as an excuse.

    QuestStand in front of the grilles and count to thirteen out loud. Then listen. Nobody may say what they heard until you're back at the gate. Whatever you heard, it was probably a goblin saying hello.
    Read aloud at the grilles

    Inside, a passage cut through the rock leads into a hall with a pointed roof and a fireplace set high in the wall. Beyond it, steps go down into the hill to an old well. People who visited by day called it cold, quiet and peaceful. Nobody has filed a report on the night shift. The falconer lived down there with his birds until 1737, and he left no diary, presumably because he was too busy having a lovely time. Wave goodbye to the goblins on your way out. They like that.

  7. 7

    The Way Home

    Go back the way you came. It's the one direction guaranteed to lead home, and the way back always feels shorter. Danskine walkers return to the lodge. Gifford walkers can finish at the Goblin Ha' Hotel in the village, which is named after the vault and comes with the splendid bonus of a roof and hot drinks.

    QuestOn the way back, nobody looks over their shoulder. Anyone who does has to walk at the back and do their best goblin impression.
