A Halloween adventure walk · Gifford, East Lothian

The Goblin Ha' Walk

A woodland ramble to Yester Castle, where a 13th-century wizard is said to have raised an army of hobgoblins to build his halls.

~3 km there and back ~1 hr walking ~100 m of climb 55.8906°N, 2.7105°W

Move your lantern over the sky to look for the castle.

Before you set out

The Goblin Ha' vault has been closed to the public since October 2021, when thieves took stone from the wall that holds it up, and the entrance lock was cut in a later break-in. You can still walk to the castle and look at the ruins from outside for free. Stay out of the tunnel and vault, and check the latest access news before you go.

The legend

The Wizard of Yester

Around 1155 the barony of Yester was granted by King Malcolm IV, and William the Lion confirmed it to a Norman family called Giffard. The name Yester comes from the old Brythonic word ystrad, meaning a valley or strath. Latin clerks spelled it "Zester".

The most famous Giffard was Sir Hugo de Giffard, a guardian of the boy-king Alexander III, who died in 1267. His neighbours whispered that he was a warlock and a necromancer. They said he struck a bargain with the Devil, who lent him a crew of hobgoblins, and that these goblins dug the great vaulted cavern under the castle in a single night.

Old tales tell that his castle, or at least the cave and keep, were wrought by witchcraft.Paraphrasing the 15th-century chronicler Walter Bower, who called the cavern "Bo' Hall"

Four hundred years later Sir Walter Scott put Hugo into his 1808 poem Marmion. In Canto III the innkeeper at Gifford tells of the old lord, and Scott names him "the founder of the Goblin-Hall". The vault has been called the Goblin Ha' (say it "Haw") ever since.

The cursed pear. One more tale follows Hugo's family. He is said to have given his daughter Margaret a pear as a wedding gift and warned that if harm ever came to it, the family's luck would end. In 1692, the story goes, a new bride at Colstoun House took a bite of it, and misfortune followed. The bitten pear is said to survive there still. Nobody has found a written record of any of this, which makes it a perfect tale for a dark walk.

Eight centuries in the woods

What really happened here

  1. c.1155Malcolm IV grants the barony of Yester. William the Lion confirms it to Hugo de Giffard, a Norman settler.
  2. pre-1267The first stone castle and the underground hall are built. Goblins, according to legend.
  3. 1278King Alexander III stays at Yester around 24 May and writes letters to Edward I of England from here.
  4. 1298Alexander de Welles is killed at the Battle of Falkirk. Edward I later hands Yester lands to Adam de Welles.
  5. 1357Heiress Joanna Giffard marries a de la Haye. The Hay family has held the barony ever since.
  6. 1487John Hay becomes the first Lord Hay of Yester.
  7. 1513The 2nd Lord Hay is killed at the Battle of Flodden.
  8. May 1544English soldiers marching home from burning Edinburgh set fire to the castle, the village and the harvest.
  9. 1547The 4th Lord defends the castle, is captured at the Battle of Pinkie and spends three years in the Tower of London.
  10. Feb 1548Lord Grey of Wilton takes the castle. Two men are said to have been made to fight a duel in Haddington market place for insulting the boy-king Edward VI.
  11. 1557The family moves to a new tower house where Yester House stands today. The old castle begins to crumble.
  12. 1646The 8th Lord is made Marquess of Tweeddale.
  13. to 1737The Marquess's falconer lives and keeps his birds in the Goblin Ha'.
  14. 2021Thieves steal stone from the wall under the vault. The castle closes to visitors.

The shortest way in starts at Danskine Lodge on the B6355, about 2 miles east of Gifford (postcode EH41 4PJ). Parking there is tight, so park on a verge east past Danskine Farm and walk back to the gate opposite Danskine Loch. For a longer outing, start in Gifford, where there is plenty of parking opposite the village park, and follow the Yester path about 1.5 miles through the woods each way. Paths near the castle are confusing, so carry an OS map.

  1. 1

    The Gatehouse

    Start at Danskine Lodge, opposite the dark water of Danskine Loch. Pick up the forest track and head into the trees.

    QuestEveryone chooses a goblin name for the walk. Use it until you're back at this gate.
    Read aloud at the gate

    Long ago, the lord of Yester needed a hall under the hill, deep enough that no army could find it. Masons said it would take ten years. Sir Hugo said it would be done by morning. That night the villagers heard picks ringing in the woods, and laughter that did not sound like men.

  2. 2

    Along the Gifford Water

    The track follows the burn through the trees. It is very muddy in places, so this is where wellies earn their keep.

    QuestFind the deepest footprint in the mud. Goblin or walker? Make your case.
  3. 3

    The Waterfall and the First Bridge

    Listen for the waterfall, then cross the bridge over the Gifford Water.

    QuestEveryone falls silent for thirty seconds on the bridge. What can you hear under the water noise?
    Read aloud on the bridge

    Goblins cannot cross running water carrying anything they stole. That is why, the old folk said, the Yester goblins left their treasure on the far bank. If you find a strange stone on the path, leave it be. Someone may be coming back for it.

  4. 4

    Shank Lea Wood

    Just before the Hopes Burn, go through Shank Lea Wood and take the path that drops down to the right.

    QuestSpot a face in a tree trunk. The person who finds the grumpiest one leads the next stretch.
  5. 5

    The Hopes Water Bridge

    Keep ahead until a bridge over the Hopes Water appears on your right. Cross it. The castle mound rises ahead, hidden by trees the Marquess of Tweeddale planted in the 1800s.

    QuestWhoever first glimpses stone through the trees shouts the password: "Giffard!"
  6. 6

    Yester Castle

    Walk around the outside of the ruins. Parts of the old courtyard wall still stand about 9 metres high, and about 12 metres where the hill falls away. At the foot of the wall, a pair of arched doorways behind iron grilles leads down to the Goblin Ha'. Peer in, but don't go in. The ruins aren't maintained and the vault is closed for safety.

    QuestStand in front of the grilles and count to thirteen out loud. Then listen. Nobody is allowed to say what they heard until you're back at the gate.
    Read aloud at the grilles

    Inside, a passage cut through the rock leads into a hall with a pointed roof and a fireplace high on the wall. Beyond it, steps go down into the hill to an old well. People who came here in daylight said it was cold, quiet and peaceful. Nobody says what it's like at night. The falconer slept down there with his birds until 1737. He never wrote down what kept him company.

  7. 7

    The Way Home

    Go back the way you came. Danskine walkers return to the lodge. Gifford walkers can finish at the Goblin Ha' Hotel in the village, named after the vault.

    QuestOn the walk back, nobody looks over their shoulder. If you do, you have to walk at the back.

Field kit

Pack like a goblin hunter

Bring

  • Wellies or boots. The paths are very muddy.
  • A head torch or lantern for each person, with spare batteries.
  • Warm layers. The castle mound is chilly even in daylight.
  • An OS map or a downloaded offline map.
  • A flask of something hot and a bag of "goblin gold" chocolate coins.

Timing

  • At the end of October the sun sets around 4:30–4:40pm in East Lothian.
  • Set off by about 3pm to reach the castle in the eerie golden hour and get back to the road by dusk.
  • The woods get dark fast under the trees, long before the sky does.

Stay safe

  • Don't enter the Goblin Ha' tunnel or vault. It's closed and the wall is damaged.
  • Don't climb on the ruins. It's a protected Scheduled Ancient Monument and nothing is maintained.
  • There's no public transport within a mile, so plan how you'll get there.
  • Don't block gates or farm tracks when you park.
  • Take nothing but photos. Even a pebble from the walls is part of the monument.
